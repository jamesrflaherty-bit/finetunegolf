Frequency, not guesswork

Measured, not assumed.

A shaft's flex letter is a starting point. Flex classifications aren't standardized between manufacturers, and two shafts with the same letter can perform differently.

Our Mitchell Golf DigiFlex 2.0 measures each shaft's actual frequency in cycles per minute (cpm). That gives us an objective number to verify flex and match shafts in a set.

  • Flex verification against the printed label
  • Shaft-to-shaft comparison before you commit
  • Frequency matching across a complete set of parallel 0.370-inch tip shafts

Why it matters

Consistency is the goal.

When the shafts in a set progress consistently, each club behaves like the one before it. That makes distances easier to predict and gapping easier to trust.

Frequency is one tool among several, alongside weight, profile and head pairing. We use all of them together. Our shaft frequency guide explains the numbers.

Every degree, checked

Measured on the angle machine.

Each club is measured on our Mitchell Golf Steelclub Signature Angle Machine, adjusted to the intended specification, and then checked again.

Adjustments can be made to single clubs or complete iron sets, so gapping stays consistent from one iron to the next.

Loft & lie

Precision you can feel on the course.

Loft and lie control launch, direction and how the sole meets the turf. A few degrees can change how a set gaps and how it performs.

  • Loft and lie measured, not estimated
  • Adjusted to spec, then verified
  • Single clubs or complete iron sets

Spec drift estimator

When will your irons need a loft and lie check?

Spec drift is mechanical bending at the hosel. It comes from the ground shock of repeated impacts, especially on hard artificial mats over concrete. Forged irons are malleable and can gradually bend over a season of heavy use. Cast irons are rigid and resist bending under normal use.

Hand-built sets

From raw components to a finished set

Every build follows the same careful path, from component selection to the moment the clubs go in your bag.

1

Select

We select KZG heads and shafts based on your fitting, your preferences and your budget.

2

Measure

Shafts are checked for frequency, and loft and lie are set and verified on the angle machine.

3

Match

Components are matched for consistency. Set shafts are frequency matched using parallel 0.370-inch tip shafts, so each club progresses from the one before it.

4

Build

Clubs are hand-assembled in our shop, then handed over ready to play.
