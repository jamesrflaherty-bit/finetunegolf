Why frequency matters in a built set

The flex letter printed on a shaft doesn't tell the whole story. Flex classifications aren't standardized between manufacturers, and individual shafts can differ measurably.

Measuring each shaft lets us spot inconsistencies and match them more precisely, so the finished clubs progress consistently from one to the next.

Precision equipment

We measure. We don't assume.

Precision fitting and building depend on accurate measurement. We own two Mitchell Golf instruments, so we verify specs ourselves rather than trusting the printed label.

Mitchell Golf DigiFlex 2.0 Frequency Meter

Measures a shaft's actual frequency, not just its flex label. We use it to verify flex, compare shafts and match sets built with parallel 0.370-inch tip shafts.

Mitchell Golf Steelclub Signature Angle Machine

Measures and sets loft and lie on each club, then checks the result against the intended specification. Every adjustment is measured, not estimated.
